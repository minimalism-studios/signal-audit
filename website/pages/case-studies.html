01 / 07 SIGNAL CLASSIFICATION / MACHINE LEARNING

Splunk MLTK BIRCH Signal Audit

Challenge. Production microservices generated substantial telemetry, logs, and alerts, leaving engineers responsible for manually interpreting system behavior and determining which signals required attention.

Solution. Develop a machine-learning approach that classifies operational behavior rather than treating every anomaly as equally important.

Implementation. A cluster-based model was built using Splunk MLTK and the BIRCH algorithm. Telemetry patterns were grouped into five categories: Noise, Baseline, Spiky Signals, Persistent Degradation, and Critical Signals.

Findings. Behavioral clustering provided a way to distinguish routine activity from emerging degradation and clarify engineering escalation priorities. This approach became the technical foundation for Signal Audit.

02 / 07 PLATFORM MIGRATION / PRODUCTION READINESS

OCP Migration Dark Mode Testing

Challenge. Migrating production workloads from PCF to OpenShift introduced potential performance, reliability, and observability risks that might not be apparent from deployment readiness alone.

Solution. Use dark mode testing to examine system behavior before full production cutover.

Implementation. Evaluate production-like traffic patterns, latency, throttling, error behavior, and observability coverage against expected system behavior.

Findings. Dark mode testing provided an opportunity to identify migration risks and monitoring gaps before exposing customers to the new platform.

03 / 07 OBSERVABILITY / ALERT PRIORITIZATION

Alert Fatigue Reduction Through Signal Classification

Challenge. High alert volumes made it difficult for engineers to distinguish urgent production problems from routine operational noise.

Solution. Classify alerts according to their operational significance, persistence, urgency, and potential customer impact.

Implementation. Organize production signals into immediate, near-term, persistent, and lower-priority categories to support more appropriate escalation decisions.

Findings. Classification created a clearer framework for prioritizing engineering attention and reducing distraction from low-value alerts.

04 / 07 PRODUCTION RELIABILITY / HIDDEN DEGRADATION

Production Throttling Detection During OCP Migration

Challenge. During an OpenShift migration, subtle throttling, latency changes, and retry behavior could be mistaken for temporary migration noise despite indicating emerging production risk.

Solution. Interpret production telemetry in context to distinguish expected migration behavior from meaningful degradation.

Implementation. Review throttling events, latency patterns, retries, and platform constraints to identify where engineering investigation was warranted.

Findings. The analysis highlighted how production pressure can emerge before a visible outage and helped focus investigation on potentially consequential system behavior.

05 / 07 INCIDENT RESPONSE / OPERATIONAL OWNERSHIP

Incident Response and Operational Ownership

Challenge. During production incidents, numerous alerts, dashboards, and competing explanations made it difficult to identify responsible teams and determine the next action.

Solution. Interpret operational signals through an ownership and escalation framework.

Implementation. Review service behavior, alert context, escalation paths, and incident history to distinguish meaningful indicators from noise and identify likely areas of responsibility.

Findings. Connecting signals to operational ownership provided a clearer basis for investigation, escalation, and engineering decisions.

06 / 07 AI RELIABILITY / SYSTEMS THINKING

AI Fails Silently: A Systems Perspective

Challenge. AI systems can continue producing responses while delivering incomplete, misleading, or unsupported information, making failures difficult to detect through traditional availability monitoring.

Solution. Evaluate AI reliability through operational context, supporting evidence, and verification rather than trusting model output alone.

Implementation. Examine missing context, false confidence, operational mismatches, and verification gaps to determine when recommendations require additional evidence or human review.

Findings. AI reliability requires attention to the quality and grounding of outputs, not merely whether the underlying service remains operational.

07 / 07 AI GOVERNANCE / OPERATIONAL INTELLIGENCE

From Authority to Actionable Intelligence

Challenge. Governed AI activity must become clear operational and executive intelligence without compromising the underlying authorization decision.

Solution. Gatekeeper remains authoritative while Signal Audit provides operational interpretation and executive presentation.

Implementation. A controlled pilot evaluated governed-event integration, receipt durability, and presentation fidelity.

Findings. All 1,000 receipts were recovered after restart, and final partner review confirmed presentation fidelity across a separate four-case live checkpoint.
