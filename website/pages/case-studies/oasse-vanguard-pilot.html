Case Study 007 / OASSE × Signal Audit / Vanguard Pilot

From Authority to Actionable Intelligence.

The Vanguard Pilot demonstrated that authoritative AI governance decisions can be transformed into actionable operational and executive intelligence without compromising the integrity of the underlying decision.

Collaboration Oak & Sparrow Systems Enterprise LLC × Signal Audit
Environment Controlled Vanguard Pilot
Validated Scope Governed-event integration and executive interpretation

The Business Challenge

An enterprise governance system can produce a correct, traceable decision without making its consequences immediately clear to the people responsible for acting on it.

Technical teams may read policy, receipt, and trace identifiers. Executives need to understand what happened, whether attention is required, and what changed.

The Vanguard Pilot tested whether Gatekeeper could remain the authoritative source of governance decisions while Signal Audit independently interpreted the resulting governed activity for operational and executive audiences. The goal was not to merge the systems, but to preserve their distinct responsibilities without compromising decision integrity.

One Governed Event. Two Distinct Responsibilities.

Gatekeeper evaluates proposed actions and preserves native outcomes, external compatibility decisions, policy, trace, and receipt identity. Where authorization is not established, its governance boundary fails closed.

Signal Audit consumes partner-safe governed events and provides operational context, investigation visibility, and audience-appropriate presentation. It does not change the authoritative decision.

Derived severity, narrative, and presentation can evolve while historical authorization and its supporting evidence remain reproducible. The signed transport path was designed to preserve identity and suppress duplicate deliveries on replay.

Executive Comprehension

During a pilot working session, OASSE COO Nick Silva requested an explanation of the situation without technical language. Signal Audit founder Kwansah Madani opened the executive dashboard, presenting the governed activity from a business perspective.

When Joshua Johosky subsequently resolved the investigation, the underlying state changed and the executive view updated in real time. Gatekeeper remained the source of decision authority; Signal Audit made the consequences accessible to a different audience.

This was a qualitative observation of executive usability, not a measured reduction in investigation time or a statistical usability study. The dashboard illustrated the integration's value; it was not the defining technical achievement.

How the Pilot Was Validated

The team treated transport acceptance, semantic correctness, and executive comprehension as separate proof obligations. A successful HTTP response alone was not considered proof of downstream interpretation or presentation.

01 / Durability 1,000 / 1,000

Receipt Recovery

All 1,000 receipts were retrieved after an API restart. Native outcomes were preserved, idempotency was checked, and a tested cross-tenant receipt read returned NOT_FOUND.

02 / Transport 20 cases

Signed Webhook

Five cases each from native ALLOW, BLOCK, REVIEW, and ABSTAIN were checked for identity, signed delivery, sanitization, replay deduplication, and clean outbox behavior.

03 / Staging HTTP 202

Live Acceptance

A sanitized native ABSTAIN / external HOLD event was accepted, with one delivered event, zero pending deliveries, and zero dead letters.

04 / Semantics 4 live cases

Semantic v1.1 Panel

Fresh ALLOW, REVIEW, ABSTAIN, and BLOCK cases were reviewed. The final partner review reported no discrepancies in decision, native outcome, resolution requirement, or identifiers.

Correcting a Semantic Mismatch

The team identified a case where a native REVIEW presented as an effective external BLOCK could retain review-oriented remediation.

The repaired envelope used BLOCK / DO_NOT_EXECUTE for the effective denial while preserving the native outcome separately. The corrected live panel and companion repository validation completed successfully on October 4, 2026.

This correction reinforced the separation between native governance evidence and downstream interpretation.

Gatekeeper establishes what was authorized, denied, or held. Signal Audit establishes what that governed activity means to the people responsible for understanding and responding to it.

What the Results Establish

The central validated outcome was an architectural and operational separation: Gatekeeper remained authoritative for governance decisions, while Signal Audit made the resulting activity intelligible to operational and executive stakeholders.

Within the bounded tests, this occurred without changing the underlying governance decision. The evidence supports receipt durability, native outcome preservation, signed delivery, replay deduplication, tested tenant isolation, fail-closed semantic correction, and partner-confirmed presentation fidelity.

For Signal Audit, the pilot validated a specific governed-event integration alongside its broader operational telemetry offering. It did not test or quantify every Signal Audit capability.

Evidence Boundaries

The Vanguard Pilot was a controlled, no-fee pilot rather than a paid production deployment. Its results do not establish production-scale availability or performance, security or regulatory certification, universal efficacy, production service-level commitments, or quantified financial return.

The integration used the FAM pilot adapter. This case study does not claim migration of that path to the later Gatekeeper V2 runtime.

The original technical validation report contains additional run identifiers, internal regressions, and evidence references for controlled diligence.
